## 3. The 10-cent answer  (headline - this part does not depend on the recipe)

* **+S$0.10 to every cup sold.** At S$1.60 the ingredient margin is S$1.287/cup (80%); at S$1.70 it is S$1.387/cup (82%). Either way +10c is a net gain per cup.
* **On 6.2 cups/day: +10c = +S$0.62/day** (zero demand loss).
* **Break-even:** +10c is profitable unless demand falls by more than **5.9%** (= S$0.10 / S$1.70). That is losing ~0.4 of the 6.2 cups/day. **This threshold does not change with ingredient cost** - so the missing recipe does not block the decision; the only unknown is the demand response, which your data cannot answer.

### 4b. Demand response to +10c  (revenue/day, correct formula)

Revenue = price x cups. Old = S$1.60 x cups; New = S$1.70 x (cups after loss). Delta/day = (1.70 x D x (1 - lost%)) - (1.60 x D). The +10c is earned on the cups kept, but the full S$1.60 is lost on the cups that walk.

| cups lost | old revenue/day | new revenue/day | delta/day (S$) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.0% | 9.93 | 10.55 | +0.62 |
| 5.0% | 9.93 | 10.02 | +0.09 |
| 5.9%  <- break-even | 9.93 | 9.93 | +0.00 |
| 10.0% | 9.93 | 9.49 | -0.43 |
| 20.0% | 9.93 | 8.44 | -1.49 |

> **Break-even = 5.9% demand loss** (S$0.10/S$1.70), independent of ingredient cost. Above that, the raise loses money. A 10% demand drop (a very plausible reaction to a 6% price rise) would cost about S$0.43/day - i.e. the raise would **not** pay off. This is why the demand response is the decision-critical unknown, and why a monitored price test is the safe path.

## 6. Plain-English conclusion

Raising Kopi by S$0.10 (S$1.60 -> S$1.70) **adds S$0.10 to every cup sold** - about **S$0.62/day** at current volume - and it only loses money if you lose more than **5.9%** of kopi cups. Because the +10c is pure margin on each cup, the ingredient cost (which we could not confirm) does not change this: even at a pessimistic S$0.45/cup cost the raise is a net gain per cup. The real risk is **demand**, which the data cannot measure. **Recommendation: a monitored, reversible price test** (one outlet, 4-6 weeks, track cups/day and revenue/day) rather than a blind chain-wide jump.
